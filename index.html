Cover Letter

It was the last morning of the programme. We were descending from a mountain refugio in the Italian Alps. Just the three of us, the last to leave, when a rainstorm arrived without warning. We did not know if the rest of the group had made it down safely. There was, however, nothing to do but walk carefully, together, through the rain. We made it down safely. And that evening on the train back to the city, we had the most honest conversation of the entire week, about what it means to feel safe with people, about how trust gets built not through icebreakers but through something so much more unplanned.

I have thought about that moment many times since. It taught me more about group dynamics than anything I could ever read, and it confirmed something I already believed: that real engagement, the kind that changes how people think and what they are willing to say, requires more than a well-designed agenda, so much more.

I built that programme, ROZMOVA, from scratch, and not because it was an assignment, but because I needed it personally. In 2022, I was a Ukrainian living in Italy, far from home, following the war in my head as much as on the news. I kept thinking about how many others like me, people who wanted to act, to organise, to do something, were simply cut off from any community that could hold them. So I applied for a fellowship, received a €25,000 grant from Robert Bosch Stiftung, opened a call, recruited 17 participants from 7 EU countries, designed the programme, and facilitated it myself. The network has been running independently ever since, with gatherings in Berlin, Lisbon, and Malta.

That experience is why the Centre for Collective Intelligence's (CCI) work feels personally meaningful to me, not just professionally interesting. Deliberative democracy is not an abstract concept, not anymore, when you come from a country where having a real say, in absolutely anything, has never been guaranteed. The question of how you create conditions for people to speak honestly, to be heard, and to actually influence what happens next is one I have been living, not just studying.

I bring to this role a mix of: facilitation experience (including a year-long qualification from Horizontal School in Berlin), qualitative research and knowledge product development from my work at UNDP's Istanbul Regional Hub, project and team management from LABA, and a Master's in International Cooperation from the University of Torino. I work with digital and AI tools as a natural part of my practice. And I am drawn specifically to CCI at this moment, to a team scaling globally, still close enough to its founding energy to be shaped by the people who join it.

I would love to bring what I have learned; from programmes that worked, and rainstorms that were not in the plan, all to this team.

Kindly,

Ivanna Sysak
